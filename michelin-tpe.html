Quantifying the Energy Cost of Rolling Resistance

Electric LCVs are far more sensitive to rolling resistance than combustion vehicles, but nobody had quantified exactly how much. I pulled real-world CAN bus data from fleet vehicles and isolated the tire-driven component of energy consumption — finding that every 1 kg/t shift in rolling resistance moves energy consumption by roughly 3–4%.

That number became the basis for a controlled field study comparing fuel economy and total cost of ownership across a 3% RR reduction, and for benchmarking 8 performance parameters against BEE Star regulatory thresholds — turning a lab-level insight into something the commercial team could act on.

Making the Models Trustworthy Enough to Use

Simulation models are only useful if people trust them. I normalized fuel consumption data from 24 trucks against gross vehicle weight to isolate tire effects cleanly, then refined parameters and filtered out real-world anomalies — pushing model-to-data agreement from 62.5% up to 91.7%.

I also moved the entire RR diagnostics pipeline from manual Excel post-processing to Python, so batch analysis across large test datasets that used to take days now runs automatically.

Getting Seven Teams to Use the Same Numbers

A model is only as useful as the number of people who trust and use it. I built 7 VECTO-based energy models to quantify TCO impact across different vehicle designs and usage conditions, then deployed a tool internally called "TCO2" — and personally trained 7 teams to standardize how performance assessments were done across the org, instead of everyone using their own spreadsheet.
